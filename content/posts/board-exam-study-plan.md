Two months is enough time to prepare well — if you use it with a plan. This one is built around three phases: cover, revise and practise.

## Phase 1: Cover the syllabus (Days 1–30)

- List every chapter in every subject.
- Mark each one **green** (confident), **yellow** (needs work) or **red** (not started).
- Study red chapters first, two subjects a day, in focused 45-minute blocks.

## Phase 2: Revise (Days 31–45)

- Make one-page summaries for each chapter: formulas, dates, definitions and diagrams.
- Teach a topic aloud to a friend or family member. If you can explain it, you know it.
- Revisit yellow chapters until they turn green.

## Phase 3: Practise (Days 46–60)

- Solve previous years' question papers under real exam timing.
- Check your answers honestly and note repeated mistakes.
- Practise writing neatly and presenting answers in points.

## Daily habits that help

1. **Sleep 7–8 hours.** Memory is built while you sleep.
2. **Put the phone in another room** while studying.
3. **Take short breaks** — a 10-minute walk beats an hour of scrolling.
4. **Eat simple, regular meals** and drink enough water.

## The night before

Revise only your summaries, pack your hall ticket and stationery, and sleep early. You've done the work — trust it.

> Consistency beats intensity. Two focused hours every day will take you further than one all-nighter.
