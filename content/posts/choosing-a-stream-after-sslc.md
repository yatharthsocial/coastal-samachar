SSLC results bring relief — and then a big question: which stream next? The choice shapes the next two years of study and many career paths after that. Here's how to make it with a clear head.

## Start with what you enjoy

Look back at the last two years. Which subjects did you look forward to? Which ones did you study even when no exam was coming? Interest keeps you going when the syllabus gets tough.

## Understand what each stream offers

### Science

Physics, chemistry and mathematics or biology. It opens doors to engineering, medicine, pure sciences, architecture and many technology careers.

### Commerce

Accountancy, business studies, economics and often mathematics or statistics. It leads towards chartered accountancy, banking, management, finance and entrepreneurship.

### Arts (Humanities)

History, political science, sociology, economics, psychology, languages and more. It prepares students for law, journalism, civil services, teaching, design and social sciences.

## Questions to ask yourself

1. Which careers excite me today, and which streams lead there?
2. Am I comfortable with the maths load in science or commerce?
3. Am I choosing this because of my interest, or because my friends are?
4. Does the college I'm considering offer the subject combination I want?

> There is no "best" stream. There is only the stream that is best for *you*.

## Talk to people who've been there

Seniors, teachers and professionals in the fields you're curious about can tell you what the day-to-day really looks like. Most are happy to help.

## For parents

Encourage, don't decide. Students who choose their own stream are far more likely to stay motivated through PUC and beyond.

## It's not a life sentence

Many careers — management, law, design, media, civil services — accept students from every stream. If you choose differently later, there are always paths forward.
