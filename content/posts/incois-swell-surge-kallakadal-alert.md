The **Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services (INCOIS)** issued high-wave and **swell surge (Kallakadal)** warnings for southern coastal regions, advising people to stay away from beaches and fishermen to stay off the sea.

## Where the alert applied

The red alert covered all coastal districts of Kerala — from Thiruvananthapuram to Kasaragod — along with parts of southern Tamil Nadu and Lakshadweep, from 26 to 28 September, [Indian Vartha reported](https://indianvartha.com/kerala-coast-on-red-alert-over-high-waves-swell-surge/). Waves of 1 to 1.5 metres were expected along the Kerala coast.

## What is Kallakadal?

Kallakadal is a sudden swelling of the sea caused by long-period waves from distant storms in the southern Indian Ocean. It can flood the shore without any local wind or rain warning.

## Safety advice

- **Avoid beaches** and sea-based activities until the warning is withdrawn.
- **Fishermen** should not take small boats out, and should keep vessels securely anchored in harbours.
- **Follow official updates** from [INCOIS](https://incois.gov.in/) and the local administration.
